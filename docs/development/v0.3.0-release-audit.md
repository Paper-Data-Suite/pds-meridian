## Calculation-state and silent-zero audit — CONFORMS

The first substantive audit pass challenged the Grade calculation layers at
the boundary where missing or unresolved academic state could accidentally
become numeric zero.

Inspected runtime families:

```text
meridian/grade_policy.py
meridian/conventional_grade.py
meridian/standards_grade.py
meridian/standards_grade_profile.py
meridian/hybrid_grade.py
```

The policy vocabulary is closed. Numeric `zero` is an explicit state
consequence only for `missing` and `incomplete`; it is rejected for pending,
excused, excluded, not-applicable, insufficient-evidence, unavailable,
withdrawn, invalid, and unresolved states.

Zero is therefore a policy consequence, not a fallback for unknown/non-Grade
state.

Direct Issue #61 adversarial coverage now walks the complete eleven-state
non-Grade vocabulary through both conventional and standards Grade
calculators under explicit exclusion. Every state preserves its source-state
identity, carries no fabricated earned/possible/conversion value, and yields
a nonnumeric insufficient final outcome when no real calculable evidence
remains.

The two zero-capable states are separately exercised under explicit `zero`
policy. Conventional Grade calculation creates a visible policy-owned zero
with the configured denominator and retained source state. Standards Grade
calculation may create a zero calculation contribution, but that contribution
does not increment `actual_calculated_result_count` and therefore cannot
satisfy minimum real-result evidence by itself.

Profile-constrained calculation was challenged with one real Meeting result
and one missing result under explicit zero treatment. The numeric zero did
not fabricate a proficiency level: the higher count predicate remained
indeterminate, its unknown standard remained unknown, and the matched
fallback band was not silently selected past that uncertainty.

Hybrid calculation was challenged with the same underlying missing state in
two policies. Under explicit zero treatment, the conventional component is a
genuinely calculated zero and retains its configured hybrid weight. Under
explicit exclusion, the conventional component remains nonnumeric
`insufficient`, is excluded, and the remaining active component is
renormalized. The hybrid layer does not turn an insufficient component into
zero on its own.

## Selection and authority audit — CONFORMS

The second substantive audit pass challenged every reviewed boundary where
Meridian could accidentally infer academic authority from sequence, score,
publication lifecycle, or result revision order.

Inspected runtime families:

```text
meridian/attempt_selection.py
meridian/attempt_selection_storage.py
meridian/attempt_decision_selection_workflow.py
meridian/reassessment.py
meridian/reassessment_storage.py
meridian/calculation_result_selection_workflow.py
meridian/academic_period_result_selection_workflow.py
meridian/ingestion.py
meridian/conventional_grade.py
```

Attempt-selection policy accepts only `selection_basis="explicit"`.
Direct Issue #61 tests reject `latest`, `highest`, and `newest` as attempted
selection bases. Multiple native point observations remain `unresolved`
rather than ranking themselves by score or sequence.

An explicit attempt decision may select an earlier/lower sequence while a
later sequence remains a candidate. Candidate order therefore describes the
observed set; it is not preference authority.

Attempt selection is bound to an exact projection snapshot. A changed source
publication identity makes the previously selected decision
`candidate_set_stale` and non-operative. Core publication supersession and
withdrawal remain publication lifecycle states (`historical`,
`withdrawn_head`, and related canonical states); they do not themselves
create replacement/reassessment authority.

Reassessment policy likewise accepts only
`relationship_basis="explicit"`. When more than one attempt is selected and
no reassessment decision exists, resolution returns `no_decision` with no
contributing attempts. Explicit replacement is direction-owned by the
decision: a lower-sequence attempt can replace a higher-sequence attempt,
demonstrating that sequence is not silently interpreted as recency or merit.

Grade Item and Academic Period result-selection workflows require an exact
persisted `result_revision`. Their `target_is_latest` fields are descriptive
only: an older historical revision can be previewed and selected explicitly
while a newer revision exists. Preview performs no authoring action, and
commit paths remain CAS-bound to the reviewed current selector.

## Override authority and source binding audit — CONFORMS

The third substantive audit pass challenged teacher final-Grade overrides at
the boundaries where override authority could otherwise drift onto a
different Grade result, bypass source freshness, or become active merely by
being authored.

Inspected runtime families:

```text
meridian/teacher_grade_override.py
meridian/teacher_grade_override_storage.py
meridian/teacher_grade_override_workflow.py
meridian/teacher_grade_override_lifecycle.py
meridian/effective_grade.py
```

Every override is bound to an exact family-tagged immutable source Grade
result reference. That reference includes the persisted result revision and
content digest. Direct Issue #61 tests confirm that an override
does not float to a later result revision even when the later Grade value is
identical,
and that changing only the source digest also makes the override inapplicable.

Exact override binding does not manufacture currentness. A matching override
against a stale selected source remains `source_result_stale` and yields no
effective Grade authority. The selected override reference must also bind the
exact immutable override decision bytes; a digest mismatch fails closed.

Override authoring and current selection remain separate. The teacher-facing
selection workflow refuses historical reselection and permits only the latest
authored immutable decision to become current. Authoring a withdrawal writes a
new immutable decision but leaves the existing current selector unchanged
until that exact withdrawal revision is explicitly selected.

Withdrawal is itself exact authority, not deletion. It must reference the
specific active override revision and digest being withdrawn. Once selected,
withdrawal returns precedence only to a current calculable base Grade; it does
not turn a stale, blocked, or otherwise non-authoritative base into a Grade.

Core publication withdrawal remains a separate producer/source lifecycle
concept from teacher override withdrawal. No reviewed override path interprets
publication withdrawal as a teacher override withdrawal or vice versa.

## Historical explanation and provenance audit — CONFORMS

The fourth substantive audit pass challenged Grade explanations at the
current-versus-historical boundary. The audit looked specifically for any
path that could explain a persisted Grade using today's policy, today's
nested proficiency selection, or newly assembled component state instead of
the exact state recorded by that Grade result.

Inspected runtime families:

```text
meridian/current_grade_preview.py
meridian/grade_preview_explanation.py
meridian/conventional_grade_explanation.py
meridian/standards_grade_explanation.py
meridian/hybrid_grade_explanation.py
meridian/grade_preview_comparison.py
```

Current preview and historical reconstruction are intentionally separate.
The current-preview witness captures current result, policy-basis, activation,
override, and freshness authority and is captured again after explanation
construction. If that witness changes, the preview fails with a currentness
conflict rather than returning an explanation assembled across two moments.

The historical authority used to explain the selected persisted Grade is
loaded from the exact activation and policy references embedded in the Grade
result. The loader uses the referenced revision numbers and verifies their
digests; it does not substitute the current activation or policy revision.
Historical policy digest drift therefore fails closed.

Conventional Grade explanation resolves each Grade Item by the exact item
revision and digest named by the persisted policy basis. A different digest
is an integrity failure rather than permission to explain from a newer item.

Standards Grade explanation requests every nested Academic Period proficiency
trace with `selection="revision"` and the exact historical result revision
recorded by the Grade result. The returned nested trace is then checked for
exact result digest, algorithm version, calculation fingerprint, target scale,
and calculated proficiency. Digest or fingerprint drift fails closed.

Hybrid Grade explanation recalculates its component checks only from the
component inputs embedded in the persisted hybrid snapshot. Those component
results must reproduce the recorded algorithm/fingerprint and Grade outcome;
the explanation layer does not reassemble components from current source
state.

Grade-preview comparison consumes frozen semantic observations. Input or
calculation-fingerprint changes that cannot be mapped to an explicit semantic
change reason are rejected rather than under-explained.

## ReportingSnapshot current-use audit — CONFORMS

The fifth substantive audit pass challenged the boundary between frozen
reporting history, explicit report current-use selection, and live academic
authority.

Inspected runtime families:

```text
meridian/reporting_snapshot.py

meridian/reporting_snapshot_storage.py
meridian/reporting_snapshot_freeze.py
```

Freezing or storing a ReportingSnapshot creates immutable reporting history
only. Neither operation creates a current-use selector. The freeze path
observes the live Grade report twice and refuses to persist a mixed-time
candidate when whole-report or row-level currentness changes.

Predecessor metadata does not move current use. `supersedes`, `corrects`, and

expected selector or wrong snapshot digest fails closed. Explicit selection
may intentionally return to an older historical snapshot; creation time and
predecessor order therefore never become implicit selection authority.

Current-use selection does not create Grade authority. Selecting or later
replacing a report snapshot does not rewrite its frozen row status,
observation, explanation, provenance, or source Grade state. Historical
snapshot bytes and digest remain unchanged after later selections.

Comparison is descriptive and snapshot-neutral. A frozen observation is used
as the prior basis, while the caller supplies live current observations. The
comparison layer reports semantic movement without recalculating, selecting,
or mutating the historical ReportingSnapshot.

## Export and source-mutation audit — CONFORMS

The sixth substantive audit pass challenged report export at its teacher
approval, final revalidation, artifact-delivery, and receipt boundaries. It
also checked that export cannot become a back door for changing academic
source state.

Inspected runtime families:

```text
meridian/export_profile.py
meridian/export_profile_storage.py
meridian/report_export_roster.py
meridian/report_export_preview.py
meridian/report_export_commit.py
meridian/report_export_receipt.py
```

Export preview is an exact-reference representation step. It loads the
digest-bound ReportingSnapshot and Export Profile supplied by the caller and,
only when the selected profile requires it, observes the bounded selected Core
roster fields. It does not read current snapshot/profile selectors, recalculate
Grades, select academic results, or write academic state. Rebuilding the same
exact sources produces the same preview bytes and payload bytes without any
workspace write.

Teacher approval is a byte-boundary. Commit rebuilds the preview from its exact
snapshot/profile references immediately before delivery. Any material change,
including a selected roster-field change, produces a currentness conflict
before an artifact or receipt is created.

File delivery is create-only/non-overwriting. An existing exact file may be
verified and reused, but an existing different file is left unchanged and the
commit fails before receipt creation. Copyable-text delivery returns exact UTF-8
text to the caller; Meridian performs no clipboard or external-system action.

A successful commit changes only the requested local artifact and Meridian's
immutable `reporting_exports` receipt state. Direct Issue #61 tests compare all
other workspace file bytes before and after commit and confirm that the frozen
ReportingSnapshot, Export Profile, and their academic source state are not
mutated.

The ExportReceipt records what Meridian exported: exact snapshot/profile and
optional roster observation, preview/payload digests, representation, safe
destination identity, actor, rationale, and export time. Its exact schema has
no external-system acceptance or acknowledgement field. A receipt is therefore
local export provenance, not evidence that a gradebook or other external
system accepted, imported, or applied the data.

## Arithmetic, denominator, and rounding audit — CONFORMS AFTER CORRECTION

The eighth substantive audit pass challenged Decimal determinism, denominator
and weight semantics, renormalization, final-only rounding, and Grade range
behavior across conventional, standards-based, and hybrid Grades.

This pass found one release correction. Conventional Grade arithmetic was
Decimal-only but inherited the caller's ambient Decimal precision. Repeating
fractions could therefore produce different stored `final_fraction` and
`unrounded_grade` values for the same exact calculation basis when an embedding
process changed `decimal.getcontext().prec`. Standards and hybrid calculations
already established their own deterministic local precision.

The correction wraps conventional Grade arithmetic in a deterministic local
Decimal context derived from the exact input magnitudes, with a compatibility
minimum of 28 digits. The public `ConventionalGradeItemInput.percentage`
projection receives the same ambient-context isolation. No binary float is
introduced into Grade-affecting arithmetic.

Denominator and weight behavior remains policy-specific and explicit. Total
points removes excluded items from both numerator and denominator, while an
explicit policy zero retains policy-owned possible points. Conventional
weighted-item/category exclusions do not silently redistribute configured
weights. Standards and hybrid active-weight normalization occurs only after the
activated state treatment explicitly classifies a source/component as
`exclude`; an explicit `zero` remains active and retains its configured weight.

Rounding remains final-only. Standards and hybrid already preserve exact
unrounded component values through their weighting steps, and hybrid combines
